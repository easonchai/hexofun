# A withdrawal is requested during the epoch and paid after it ends

> **2026-10-04: superseded by [ADR 0017](0017-live-yield-index-and-instant-withdraw.md).**
> Withdraw is instant from an Idle band and the venue, with the Tickets rule restored for the
> instant path. The request and process pair below stays as the queued fallback.

`withdraw` debited the Player and transferred USDC in the same instruction, which only works while the whole vault sits in the principal vault. Real yield means the principal is lent out for most of the epoch and comes back shortly before the draw, so for most of the day the vault cannot pay. The rebuild splits the instruction in two. `request_withdraw(amount)` checks `principal >= amount`, deducts `amount` from `principal` and `total_principal`, deducts `min(entries, amount)` from `entries`, adds the amount to `player.pending_withdraw` and `pool.pending_withdrawals`, sets `pending_epoch = pool.current_epoch_id`, moves no tokens, and is never blocked by `paused`. `process_withdraw` takes no signer at all, and a request matures on either of two rules: `pool.current_epoch_id > player.pending_epoch`, or `now > player.requested_at + pool.epoch_seconds`. The second exists because only the operator can call `begin_epoch`, so without it a stalled operator would freeze every pending withdrawal; it can never fire first while the operator is alive, because the epoch the request landed in ends within `epoch_seconds` of the request. Once either holds, anyone may push the payout, and it transfers the whole pending amount from the vault to the owner's token account. When the vault is short it fails with `InsufficientVaultLiquidity`, the operator retries every tick, and `/status` reports the shortfall. The old `entries >= amount` rule is dropped, because the payout lands after the boundary where Entries would have reset to Principal anyway, so a player who lost every Entry in rounds is no longer locked out for an extra day.

Considered and rejected: a withdrawal window, a few minutes around the epoch boundary where `withdraw` still transfers directly. It keeps one instruction, but it makes the depositor show up at a clock time, and missing it costs a full extra epoch. It also gives the UI a second deadline to explain, and it is no safer than the request, since the money is either back from the venue at the boundary or it is not.

Considered and rejected: a liquidity buffer, holding back a percentage of the vault so small withdrawals stay instant. The buffer earns nothing, its size is a guess against unknown demand, and the first day it empties the product has to explain why the same button paid one depositor at once and another the next day. One behaviour is worth more than the convenience.

Considered and rejected: a PDA per request, which would allow several outstanding requests and a cancel. That is rent and an account creation per withdrawal for a feature nobody asked for. Two `u64` fields on the Player cover the case at no extra account, and the price is that there is one pending amount per Player, so a second request merges into the first and moves the whole amount to the later epoch.

Consequences: cash arrives up to one epoch after the request, and the vault screen has to say so before the button is pressed, with the pending amount, the epoch it pays in, and a link that sends `process_withdraw` when the operator has not. The operator gains a step after payout that pushes every matured request. ADR 0002 chose on-chain registration partly because it left withdrawals unblocked; that still holds of registration, and the delay now comes from custody instead. PRD §3.1's "never blocked by pause, epoch state, or operator action" is superseded, except for the operator, whom no depositor has to wait for.
