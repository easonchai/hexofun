# A pool can change its venue behind a 48 hour lock, and a Kamino Earn vault is an allowed venue

Status: accepted (2026-10-07). It amends ADR 0017 in two places: the four venue pins are no longer permanent, and a Kamino Earn vault (kVault) is no longer rejected. The spec is `docs/plan/venue-switch/spec.md`. ADR 0016 still decides everything the padding cannot hold, and this change needed no layout break.

ADR 0017 pinned one klend reserve at `create_pool` and said no caller can substitute another. That kept the pool's USDC away from any address the Admin could name, and it also meant a pool could never leave its first venue. The SOL/BTC market's USDC reserve paid 4.5% at 92% utilization on 2026-10-07. Another klend market's USDC reserve paid 5.8%, and the RockawayX RWA USDC vault paid about 5.4% before its 5% performance fee. The only way to follow a better venue, or to leave a bad one, was a Pool cutover with every depositor moving their own money. ADR 0017 also rejected Earn vaults, because the curator can change fees and set a withdrawal penalty with no time lock.

This ADR keeps the first rule where it matters and drops it where it did not. No instruction sends pool USDC to an outside address, and none ever will. What changes is which venue the pool points at, and that change now waits out a lock the program enforces.

The proposal. The Admin calls `propose_venue` with a venue kind and the four pubkeys. The program creates a `VenueProposal` account at the PDA `["venue_proposal", pool]` and records the time. Proposing again replaces the open proposal and restarts the lock, the way `propose_admin` replaces a pending admin. `cancel_venue` is Admin only and closes it. The proposal has no expiry. Once the lock has elapsed it stays executable until someone cancels or executes it. The account is separate because the Pool's 88 padding bytes cannot hold four pubkeys, and a Pool realloc would change the layout test for nothing.

The lock is the constant `VENUE_LOCK_SECONDS`, 48 hours. It is not a parameter, so shortening it takes an upgrade that depositors can see. A queued withdrawal needs an epoch boundary to pay, so 24 hours gives a depositor no real window, and 7 days is too slow for a venue incident. Deposits and withdrawals keep working throughout. While a proposal exists, `deploy_to_venue` refuses with `VenueProposalOpen`, so a drain cannot be undone by the Operator's own crank. The Operator's tick reads that error as a skip.

The drain. `drain_venue(amount)` runs Sync, then redeems `amount` from the current venue into the principal vault, and lowers the venue value by what arrived. It is repeatable and partial. The USDC lands in the principal vault and nowhere else, which is why the permissions are wide. The Admin or the Operator may drain at any time with no proposal. Anyone may drain while a proposal exists, so the Operator's crank needs no admin signature and a lost admin key cannot leave a proposal stuck. A klend reserve that cannot cover the amount fails with the existing `VenueIlliquid` and nothing moves. A kVault that returns less than asked books only what returned. Any gap between the value a drain removed and the USDC that arrived is booked against `fee_owed`, floored at zero, the rule `recall` already uses.

The emergency park. A drain with no proposal is how the team leaves a venue that is going bad. The admin CLI's `drain-venue` sets the deploy pause in the same transaction, before the drain, so the Operator cannot put the USDC straight back. Funds sit idle and earn nothing until the team proposes a new venue and the lock passes. The lock applies to moving money somewhere new, not to moving it home.

The Operator's tick drains only once the lock has elapsed, and not at proposal time. Draining at proposal time would put the whole position at 0% for 48 hours on every move. Depositors' ninth story in the spec asks that a proposal not cost two days of yield, so the pool keeps earning at the old venue until the lock ends. Ticket 04 worded this loosely, and the tick follows the story. After the lock, the tick drains in steps sized by the old venue's free liquidity, then migrates.

The migration. `migrate_venue` is permissionless. It needs an open proposal whose lock has elapsed and an old position worth at most `min_venue_move`. It runs Sync, repins the kind and the four pubkeys from the proposal, sets the venue value to zero, closes the proposal into the Admin who paid its rent, and emits `VenueMigrated`. It moves no USDC. The idle USDC the drains produced goes to the new venue through the ordinary deploy crank, under the existing idle band and deploy caps, which read the new venue from then on. The gap between the last drain and the first deploy costs yield, and the Operator keeps it short.

Left behind. Dust the old venue would not redeem is allowed through, so a few base units cannot block a migration forever. The migration leaves up to `min_venue_move` untracked at the old venue. The event reports it as `left_behind`. The pool books nothing against it and writes nothing off. The default `min_venue_move` is 100 USDC, so this is a real amount, not rounding. A full drain avoids it, and the runbook drains to zero before it migrates.

The kVault backing. The venue module gains a second backing behind the same three operations, value, deposit and redeem, and dispatches on a venue kind byte taken from the Pool's padding. Zero is klend, so every existing pool reads as klend. One is the localnet stub, and two is a kVault. The four pins keep their names on chain, because renaming breaks every client's IDL. For a kVault they are the kVault program, the vault state, the shares mint and the pool's shares token account. `create_pool` checks them against the vault state, and every call refuses a reserve list that does not match the allocations the state holds, with `VaultAccountMismatch`. The published interface is used and nothing is copied from the BUSL source. The production build serves both kinds, and the `test-venue` feature still adds the stub.

The vault values the position conservatively. Value is the pool's shares times the vault's assets under management over shares issued, after the program refreshes every allocation reserve itself. The vault keeps 1,000 base units of every withdraw, flat. The program takes `max(1000, min_withdraw_amount) + 10` off the value, about 1,010 base units under the formula, and prices every redeem with it, so a full redeem returns at least the value. A deposit is also booked about 13 base units under what was paid, and the program leaves that gap alone because it errs the safe way. The fork measured 1,023 short on a 9,000 USDC deploy. It also takes off the performance and management fees the vault has not yet charged, so a vault nobody touched for a day does not overstate the pool's share. Measured cost on the fork, whole transaction, in compute units: deploy 303k to 311k, recall 341k, harvest 363k, and the largest redeem 400k, inside the 600k the Operator simulates under. The largest transaction is 612 bytes against the 1,232 limit, using the vault's own lookup table.

Rewards. A kVault farm's rewards accrue unclaimed. Nothing claims them. A Treasury sweep of those rewards is the follow-up if the amount justifies it.

Curator risk, accepted. ADR 0017's reasons for rejecting vaults still hold. The vault's admin can raise the performance fee and set a withdrawal penalty with no time lock, and the allocation admin can move the vault's money between markets. The team accepts that in exchange for the curator's allocation work, which this team does not want to build. What protects depositors is that a pool is never forced into a vault. Entering one is a proposal that depositors see 48 hours ahead, and leaving one needs no lock. The deploy caps still bound exposure. The pool may hold at most 5% of a vault's assets, and the vault must have withdrawable liquidity to cover a floor.

The first target is the RockawayX RWA USDC vault, `DWSXb18xZApz29vnQpgR2m6MynCT7PznaXt7Ut7M7KaP`, with a 500 bps performance fee, no management fee and seven allocation slots in six markets, mostly Huma and OnRe. The spec does not say when a mainnet pool moves. It delivers the mechanism and a fork rehearsal.

The rate gap is not free yield. The vault pays more than the SOL/BTC reserve because its allocations lend against real-world-asset collateral, and a default there falls on the vault's depositors. Moving from a crypto-collateral reserve to that vault trades liquidity and collateral risk for rate. The pool also absorbs the vault's withdrawal mechanics, a flat 1,000 base unit cut and a source reserve that can run short.

The switch exists for strategic moves, not rate chasing. Every move costs a position at zero while it drains, a gap before the first deploy and the old venue's exit friction, and the spread between venues is not worth that for a few tenths of a percent. A proposal needs a reason a depositor would accept, such as a reserve being deprecated, a curator changing terms, or a gap wide enough to survive the risk above.

Rejected: holding positions at two venues, or a weighted split. A pool that wants spread across markets deposits into a kVault, which does the rebalancing. Rejected: a shorter lock or an emergency path that moves funds to a new venue without it. A stolen admin key could use that path to point the pool at a bad venue on the spot.

Consequences: `create_pool` and every Sync still check their accounts against the pins on the Pool, and the pins are now what `migrate_venue` rewrites. The admin CLI gains `propose-venue`, `cancel-venue`, `drain-venue` and `migrate-venue`, all in local, Ledger and Squads signing. Propose and cancel stay in the CLI, where the Squads flow is. The ops console shows the proposal and offers drain and migrate once the chain allows them. The indexer records the four events, proposed, cancelled, drained and migrated. Errors are appended, never reordered, and the IDL copies in the backend and the web are regenerated. No depositor-facing screen changes, and a banner for an open proposal waits until the team decides to show one. Pools ship the venue kind byte in padding, so no migration is needed.
